## REFERENCE
Article 158 of the Egyptian Civil Code allows parties to agree that, in case of non-performance, the contract is rescinded automatically without a court order — but the obligation to serve a formal summons still applies, unless the parties expressly dispense with it too.

Article 158 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides:

* **Subject** — Express resolution clauses (clauses résolutoires) in contracts: stipulations that the contract dissolves upon non-performance.
* **Default Egyptian rule (background)** — Without an express clause, rescission for non-performance requires a court order; this protects parties from peremptory dissolution.
* **What Article 158 permits** — Parties may agree that, on non-performance, the contract is deemed rescinded ipso facto (by the fact itself) without a court order.
* **Effect of such agreement** — Rescission becomes automatic; the aggrieved party can treat the contract as ended without filing for judicial dissolution.
* **Continuing requirement: formal summons** — The agreement does not, by default, release the parties from serving a formal summons before rescission takes effect.
* **Why summons is still required** — Protects the breaching party from surprise; gives them an opportunity to cure (perform) before automatic rescission bites.
* **Express dispensation possible** — Parties may also expressly agree to dispense with the formal summons; only then does true ipso facto rescission apply, without notice.
* **Practical implication for drafters** — A simple "automatic rescission" clause requires summons; for true no-summons rescission, drafters must add explicit language dispensing with summons.
* **Practical implication for breaching parties** — The article preserves a window of cure unless explicitly closed by contract.

Example: A supply contract includes an "automatic rescission on non-payment" clause. The buyer fails to pay. Under Article 158, the seller may treat the contract as rescinded without going to court, but only after serving a formal summons giving the buyer a chance to pay. To skip the summons, the contract would need to expressly say no summons is required.

## BASELINE (Stage 0) PREDICTION
Article 158 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides that a person who has been injured by another's negligence may recover damages from the negligent party even if the injury was not caused by the negligent act itself but rather by an intervening cause.

Article 158 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides:

* **The rule**: A person who is injured by another's negligence may recover damages from the negligent party even if the injury was not caused by the negligent act itself but rather by an intervening cause.
* **The exception**: The rule does not apply if the injury was caused by the negligent act itself.
* **The rationale**: This rule reflects the principle that one should not be penalized for actions they did not commit, and it also encourages the injured party to hold the negligent party accountable for their actions.

Example: A pedestrian is hit by a car while crossing the street. The driver was not negligent; he was simply driving in a normal manner. Under Article 158, the pedestrian can still recover damages from the driver because the injury was not caused by the driver's negligence, but rather by the pedestrian's own negligence (crossing the street at a red light).

## STAGE-1 PREDICTION
Article 158 of the Egyptian Civil Code sets out the rule for the sale of goods at a price that is not fixed in advance — the so-called "open market" sale. The article establishes that the seller must deliver the goods as agreed and that the buyer has the right to reject them if they do not conform to the description or quality specified.

Article 158 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides:

* **Scope** — Applies to the sale of goods at an open market price.
* **Seller's obligation** — Must deliver the goods as agreed.
* **Buyer's rights** — Has the right to reject the goods if they do not conform to the description or quality specified.
* **Reason for the rule** — Protects both parties from unfairness; buyers cannot be forced to accept substandard goods without recourse.
* **Effect on the contract** — The buyer may reject the goods after acceptance; the seller must deliver them as agreed.
* **Practical implication** — Buyers should carefully inspect the goods before accepting them; sellers must deliver as agreed.

Example: A buyer purchases a car from a seller at an open market price. The seller agrees to deliver the car within three days. If the buyer finds the car has significant defects (e.g., a major mechanical problem), they can reject it under Article 158. The seller must then deliver the car as agreed, but the buyer has the right to reject it if it does not meet the agreed description or quality.
