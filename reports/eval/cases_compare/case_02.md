## REFERENCE
Article 1068 of the Egyptian Civil Code sets out the formal requirements for an application to force a sale by auction in mortgage purge proceedings: the form of the summons, the deposit needed at the court, the no-refund rule for an unsuccessful auction, the nullity of an application that fails to comply, and the conditions for renouncing the application.

Article 1068 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides:

* **Setting** — A creditor's application to force the sale of a mortgaged property at auction (in the context of mortgage purge proceedings against a third-party holder).
* **Form of the summons** — A summons to the third-party holder and to the former owner, signed by the applicant or by their representative who holds a special mandate for the purpose.
* **Deposit at the court Caisse** — The applicant must deposit a sum sufficient to cover the cost of the auction sale at the court's Caisse (treasury).
* **No-refund rule** — If no higher price than the third-party holder's offer is obtained at the auction, the applicant has no right to a refund of the advanced expenses.
* **Why the no-refund rule** — It discourages frivolous auction applications; an applicant who forces the auction without obtaining a higher price absorbs the cost.
* **Nullity for non-compliance** — Failure to comply with any of these conditions (form, signature, special mandate, deposit) entails the nullity of the application.
* **Renunciation requires consent** — The applicant cannot renounce the application without the consent of all the inscribed creditors and all the sureties — protecting their reliance on the auction proceeding.
* **Why renunciation requires unanimous consent** — Inscribed creditors and sureties may have made plans relying on the auction; an applicant cannot unilaterally undo their expectations.

Example: A registered creditor wants to force an auction of a mortgaged building held by a third party. Under Article 1068, they must serve formal summons on the third-party holder and former owner, deposit auction costs at the court Caisse, and accept the no-refund rule if the auction yields no higher price. Failing any condition voids the application; renouncing later requires consent from all other creditors and sureties.
