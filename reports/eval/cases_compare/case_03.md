## REFERENCE
Article 280 of the Egyptian Civil Code governs how a debtor can satisfy a debt held in solidarity by multiple creditors, and what happens to a creditor's share when they die. It balances the debtor's practical convenience against the right of any creditor to insist that payment be made to them.

Article 280 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides that, when there is solidarity between creditors, the debtor may pay the debt to any one of them unless one of them objects; and that solidarity does not prevent the debt from being divided between the heirs of one of the joint and several creditors, unless the debt itself is indivisible.

To walk through the article:

1. Default flexibility for the debtor. The general rule benefits the debtor: when several creditors share solidarity, paying any one of them in full is enough to discharge the obligation. The chosen creditor's receipt is binding on the others.

2. Veto by objection. The article explicitly preserves the right of any creditor to object to the debtor's chosen payee. Once an objection is raised, the debtor can no longer choose freely; they must direct payment differently to avoid making an invalid payment.

3. Inheritance and division. The death of a joint and several creditor breaks up that creditor's share. The share passes to the heirs as a divisible debt by default. This contrasts with the original solidarity, which is not inherited.

4. Indivisibility carries forward. If the debt itself is indivisible (for example, the delivery of a unique object), division among heirs does not work. The heirs must continue to act collectively for that share.

Example: Three joint and several creditors are owed 60,000 EGP. The debtor pays any one of them in full unless objected to. If a creditor dies leaving two heirs, that 20,000 EGP share splits 10,000 between them.

## BASELINE (Stage 0) PREDICTION
Article 280 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides that a person who has been injured in a motor vehicle accident is entitled to compensation from the insurer of the driver involved.

Article 280 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides:

* **Scope**: The provision applies only to injuries caused by a motor vehicle accident.
* **Subject matter**: The right to compensation is limited to the injury itself, not to any damage to the vehicle or other property.
* **Insurer's liability**: The insurer of the driver is responsible for compensating the injured party.
* **No limitation on amount**: The insurer must pay the full amount of the injury, regardless of the actual cost of medical treatment or rehabilitation.
* **No time limit**: The right to compensation accrues immediately upon the injury and continues until it is fully paid.
* **Provisional payment**: The insurer may make a provisional payment before the final amount is determined, but this does not affect the right to compensation.

Example: A pedestrian is hit by a car in a traffic accident. Under Article 280, the pedestrian is entitled to compensation from the car's insurer, which must pay the full amount of the injury, regardless of how much it costs to treat the injury. The insurer cannot limit its payment to the actual cost of medical treatment; it must compensate the full amount of the injury, even if the total cost exceeds the insured value of the car.

## STAGE-1 PREDICTION
Article 280 of the Egyptian Civil Code addresses the effect of a sale on the seller's obligation to deliver goods and the buyer's obligation to pay. It establishes that the buyer is not released from payment until the seller delivers the goods, and that the seller may be released from delivery if he has fulfilled his obligation to deliver.

Article 280 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides:

* **Subject matter** — The article applies to sales of movable property.
* **Seller's obligation to deliver** — The seller must deliver the goods at the time of sale.
* **Buyer's obligation to pay** — The buyer must pay for the goods at the time of sale.
* **Effect of failure to deliver** — If the seller fails to deliver, the buyer is not released from payment.
* **Effect of failure to pay** — If the buyer fails to pay, the seller may be released from delivery.
* **Why this distinction** — In a sale, the buyer's obligation to pay is conditional upon the seller delivering; the seller's obligation to deliver is conditional upon the buyer paying.
* **Practical implication** — A seller who fails to deliver can still collect payment; a buyer who fails to pay cannot claim delivery.

Example: A sells a car to B. B pays the price. Under Article 280, B is not released from payment until A delivers the car. If A fails to deliver, B cannot claim delivery; if B fails to pay, A may release delivery.
