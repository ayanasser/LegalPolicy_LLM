## REFERENCE
Article 764 of the Egyptian Civil Code regulates the consequences of incorrect particulars or misstatements about the assured's age in life insurance, generally avoiding outright nullity in favor of proportional adjustment of the sum assured or premium.

Article 764 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides:

* **Misstatement of age does not normally void the policy:** Incorrect particulars or misstatements as to the age of the life assured do not render the assurance void — except in one specific case.
* **Exception: true age above the table limit:** The policy is void only if the true age of the life assured exceeds the limit specified in the insurer's table of rates.
* **Adjustment when premium too low:** If the misstatement led to an agreed premium lower than what should have been paid, the sum assured is reduced in proportion that the agreed premium bears to the premium that should be paid based on true age.
* **Refund when premium too high:** If the agreed premium is higher than what should have been paid, the insurer must refund the excess; the assured does not lose for paying more than necessary.

Example: A 50-year-old applies for life insurance stating age 45 to get a lower premium. Years later, the misstatement is discovered. Under Article 764, the policy is not void (assuming 50 is within the rate table). The sum assured is proportionally reduced based on the ratio of agreed premium to the proper premium for a 50-year-old at the time of contracting.

## BASELINE (Stage 0) PREDICTION
Article 764 of the Egyptian Civil Code establishes that when two or more persons jointly own property, they may agree to transfer their individual interests in the same way as if they were co-owners.

Article 764 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides:

* **Joint ownership**: Two or more persons jointly own property.
* **Agreement on transfer**: They may agree to transfer their individual interests in the same way as if they were co-owners.
* **No separate title**: The transferee does not acquire a separate title; they become part of the joint owner.
* **Effect on other owners**: Other joint owners must be notified and agree to the transfer.
* **No right to rescind**: The transferee cannot later claim the original owners' rights (e.g., against third parties) because they did not consent.

Example: A partnership owns a house. They agree to sell it to a third party. Under Article 764, the third party becomes part of the partnership, not a new owner. The other partners must agree to the sale, and the partnership's rights against third parties are unaffected by the sale.

## STAGE-1 PREDICTION
Article 764 of the Egyptian Civil Code addresses the legal effect of a contract that is not in writing when the parties have agreed on its terms in writing and the contract has been performed.

Under Article 764 of the Egyptian Civil Code, if two parties to a contract agree on all its terms in writing and the contract has been performed, the contract is valid even though it was not written down. The parties' agreement is sufficient to create a legally enforceable contract.

The article provides three elements:

1. Agreement on all terms: The contract must be fully agreed upon by both parties, including all essential conditions and obligations.

2. Performance of the contract: The contract must have been carried out according to the agreed terms.

3. Validity of the contract: The absence of a written document does not render the contract void; it remains valid.

Example: Two friends agree in writing on the terms of their shared apartment — who pays what bills, how they share chores, and the rules for disputes. They live together as agreed. Under Article 764, their agreement creates a valid contract despite not being written down.
