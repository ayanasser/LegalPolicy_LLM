## REFERENCE
Article 662 of the Egyptian Civil Code grants sub-contractors and workers in a construction-type contract a direct right of action against the master (the project owner), limited to amounts the master still owes the main contractor, with a privilege over those amounts upon attachment, and priority over any assignment of those sums.

Article 662 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides:

* **Direct action by sub-contractors and workers** — Sub-contractors and workmen of the main contractor have a direct right of action against the master, but capped at the sums the master owes the main contractor on the action's commencement.
* **Same right for sub-sub-contractors' workers** — Workers of sub-contractors have the same direct right of action against the main contractor and the master.
* **Privilege upon attachment** — When one of them serves an attachment on the master or main contractor, the workers gain a privilege on the sums due to the main contractor or sub-contractor at attachment, in proportion to amounts owed each of them.
* **Direct payment** — These sums may be paid directly to the workers and sub-contractors.
* **Priority over assignees** — The rights of sub-contractors and workers under this article have priority over those of any party to whom the contractor has assigned sums due from the master.
* **Why these protections** — Sub-contractors and workers are economically vulnerable; without the article they would only have personal claims against the contractor (often insolvent), missing out on funds in the master's hands.
* **Practical effect** — Bypasses the contractor when needed, channelling funds directly to sub-contractors and workers.

Example: A property owner owes a main contractor 1,000,000 EGP. The main contractor's electricians, owed 200,000 EGP, sue the property owner directly. Under Article 662, they can recover up to 200,000 EGP from the 1,000,000 still in the owner's hands, even if the contractor has assigned the receivable to a bank.
