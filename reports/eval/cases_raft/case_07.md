## REFERENCE
Article 764 of the Egyptian Civil Code regulates the consequences of incorrect particulars or misstatements about the assured's age in life insurance, generally avoiding outright nullity in favor of proportional adjustment of the sum assured or premium.

Article 764 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides:

* **Misstatement of age does not normally void the policy:** Incorrect particulars or misstatements as to the age of the life assured do not render the assurance void — except in one specific case.
* **Exception: true age above the table limit:** The policy is void only if the true age of the life assured exceeds the limit specified in the insurer's table of rates.
* **Adjustment when premium too low:** If the misstatement led to an agreed premium lower than what should have been paid, the sum assured is reduced in proportion that the agreed premium bears to the premium that should be paid based on true age.
* **Refund when premium too high:** If the agreed premium is higher than what should have been paid, the insurer must refund the excess; the assured does not lose for paying more than necessary.

Example: A 50-year-old applies for life insurance stating age 45 to get a lower premium. Years later, the misstatement is discovered. Under Article 764, the policy is not void (assuming 50 is within the rate table). The sum assured is proportionally reduced based on the ratio of agreed premium to the proper premium for a 50-year-old at the time of contracting.

*   **Third-party interest:** A creditor can sue someone else who has a legal interest in the same debt.
*   **Legal interest:** This third party must hold the debt as a security interest (like a mortgage or a charge), not just as a general creditor.
*   **Proof of interest:** The creditor must prove they have a legal interest in the debt.
*   **Enforcement right:** Once proven, the creditor can ask the court to enforce their legal interest.
*   **Court's discretion:** The court will decide whether to enforce the legal interest based on fairness and justice.

Example: Suppose you owe your friend $10,000. Your friend has a mortgage on your house securing that debt. If you sue your friend, you can also sue the mortgagee (the bank) to enforce your legal interest in the debt. The court will then decide whether to enforce the mortgagee's interest.

This article reflects the Egyptian Civil Code's approach to protecting creditors' rights by allowing them to pursue their interests through the courts, even when those interests are held by third parties.

Article 764 of the Egyptian Civil Code addresses how incorrect information about the age of a life assured affects the validity of an insurance policy and the amount of the premium payable.

Article 764 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides rules governing the effect of incorrect information on life-insurance policies. It establishes two main principles:

1. Misstatements do not invalidate the contract — unless they exceed the limits set by the insurer's table of rates. This protects the insurer from being penalized for honest mistakes made by the applicant.

2. When the incorrect information results in a lower-than-expected premium, the policyholder's sum assured is reduced proportionally to the difference between the agreed and true ages. The insurer refunds the excess premium without interest.

3. When the agreed premium is higher than the true premium, the insurer must refund the excess and reduce future premiums to the true rate.

Example: A 30-year-old applies for a life insurance policy with a 5% premium rate. The insurer's table of rates specifies a maximum age of 60. The applicant lies about their age, claiming they're 40. The actual age turns out to be 45 when the policy lapses. Under Article 764, the policyholder's sum assured is reduced to reflect the true age (45), and the insurer refunds the excess premium (1.5% of the original premium). Future premiums will then be calculated at the true rate of 5%.
