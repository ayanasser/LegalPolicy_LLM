## REFERENCE
Article 943 of the Egyptian Civil Code lays out the strict procedural framework for an action in pre-emption (الشفعة): the parties to be sued, the competent court, the very short filing window, and the priority disposition the case receives. Failure to comply with any of these requirements forfeits the right.

Article 943 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides:

* **Parties: vendor and purchaser:** The pre-emption action must be brought against both the vendor and the purchaser of the immovable, who are necessary parties to the case.
* **Court: place of the property:** The action must be filed before the Court of the district in which the immovable is situated, not the court of the parties' residence or any other forum.
* **Strict 30-day deadline:** The case must be enrolled on the Court list within 30 days from the date of the notification provided in the preceding article. Filing outside this window forfeits the right of pre-emption.
* **Urgent disposition:** Once filed, the case will be disposed of as a matter of urgency, recognizing the legitimate interest of all parties in resolving the cloud on the title quickly.

Example: A landowner sells his property to a buyer; a neighbor with a right of pre-emption is formally notified on January 1. To exercise pre-emption, the neighbor must file suit against both vendor and buyer, in the court of the property's district, and have it enrolled on the Court list by January 30. If he files on January 31, the right is forfeited under Article 943, regardless of how meritorious the claim may have been.

Article 943 of the Egyptian Civil Code deals with the rules governing the division of a property when it is jointly owned by two or more co-owners.

Under this article, the co-owners share the property equally unless they have agreed otherwise. If they have not, and one co-owner has a superior claim to the property (such as a mortgage), that co-owner may retain ownership of part of the property and take over the debt from the other co-owners.

The article also provides that if one co-owner transfers his/her interest in the property to another person, the transferee becomes a co-owner and shares in the property proportionally to their respective interests.

In summary, Article 943 sets out the default rule for joint ownership — equal shares — but allows for superior claims and transfers, providing flexibility for the practical realities of real-world co-ownership arrangements.

Article 943 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides a procedure for actions in preemption (a right of first refusal) — the vendor's claim to prevent the purchaser from selling the property to a third party — with three elements: filing, enrollment, and urgency.

Article 943 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides:

* **Subject** — Actions in preemption (right of first refusal) between a buyer and a seller.
* **Filing requirement** — The action must be introduced against both the vendor and the purchaser before the district court where the property is situated.
* **Enrollment requirement** — The action must be enrolled on the court list within thirty days from the date of notification provided for in the preceding article.
* **Why the filing and enrollment deadlines** — To give the parties sufficient notice and protection; the court must act promptly.
* **Urgency** — The case is disposed of as a matter of urgency, meaning the court may grant interim relief if needed.
* **Why urgency** — Preemption is a right of first refusal; the court must act quickly to protect the seller's interest.

Example: A buyer buys a house from a seller who then sells it to a third party. Under Article 943, the seller can bring an action in preemption against the buyer and the third party. The buyer must file this action within 30 days of receiving notice (from the seller), and the court must decide the case as a matter of urgency.
