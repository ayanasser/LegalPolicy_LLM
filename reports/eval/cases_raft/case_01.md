## REFERENCE
Article 690 of the Egyptian Civil Code obliges the master to pay the worker his salary at the time and place agreed in the contract or established by custom, subject to mandatory provisions in special labor laws — keeping the framework simple and party-driven for matters not regulated otherwise.

Article 690 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides:

* **Master's duty to pay salary:** The master must pay the worker his salary, the basic counter-performance owed under the employment contract.
* **Time of payment: agreement, then custom:** Payment must be made at the time agreed upon in the contract; if the contract is silent, at the time established by custom (e.g., monthly in advance or arrears, weekly, etc.).
* **Place of payment: agreement, then custom:** Similarly, payment must be made at the place agreed in the contract; if the contract is silent, at the customary place.
* **Special labor laws prevail:** All these defaults are subject to mandatory provisions contained in special labor laws — modern labor statutes may impose specific timing (e.g., monthly payment by a fixed date) and place (e.g., bank transfer to the worker's account) that override contractual freedom.

Example: An employment contract is silent on when and where the salary is paid. Local industry custom is monthly payment by the 5th of the following month, in cash at the workplace. Under Article 690, those custom-established norms govern. However, if a special labor law requires bank transfers for any worker earning above a threshold, that statutory rule overrides the cash-at-workplace custom.

## RAFT CLOSED-BOOK PREDICTION (no article given)
Article 690 of the Egyptian Civil Code is a rule that regulates the effect of a contract's termination on the parties' obligations and the third party's rights.

The rule provides that when a contract is terminated before its expiration, the parties remain bound to perform their obligations up to the date of termination, unless the contract has been assigned or transferred to a third party who has not assumed the obligation.

The article applies to all types of contracts — including those for services, goods, real estate, and other agreements — and to all kinds of terminations, whether by mutual agreement, by the performance of an act, or by the occurrence of an event.

The article reflects the general principle of contractual certainty: even if a contract is terminated early, the parties should be able to rely on their obligations until the termination date, so they can plan accordingly.

The article also reflects the practical reality that many contracts are assigned to third parties after their initial formation; the rule protects these third-party beneficiaries from being penalized for the original contract's early termination.

Example: A contract for a service between two companies is terminated one day before its expiration. Under Article 690, the companies remain bound to complete the service as agreed, unless the service was already performed by the time of termination (in which case the company that performed it is entitled to claim against the other company). The third-party beneficiary of the assignment (the company that received the service) remains protected.

## RAFT OPEN-BOOK PREDICTION (oracle + 1 distractor given in context)
Article 690 of the Egyptian Civil Code sets out the rules governing payment of wages under an employment contract. The rule is threefold:

1. Salary payment at the time of the contract or customarily.

The master must pay the worker his salary at the time the contract is made or at the time established by custom, subject to the provisions of special laws. This means:

- The master pays on the date the contract is signed.
- If no contract exists, the master pays when the work is completed.
- Customary practice can establish a different payment schedule.
- Special laws override customary practices (e.g., for international workers).

Example: A contract specifies monthly payments; if the master fails to pay on time, the worker can sue for breach of contract.

2. Payment subject to special law.

The payment must also comply with any special law that applies to the specific circumstances — for example, special labour laws, tax regulations, or collective agreements.

Example: An employee works in a foreign country; special labour laws govern remuneration and protection. The master must follow those special rules.

3. Notice obligation when terminating.

If the master wishes to terminate the contract, he must notify the lessee in accordance with the delays provided for in Article 563 (which lays down the general rules for giving notice).

Example: A lease expires; the landlord must give the tenant proper notice before terminating the lease.
