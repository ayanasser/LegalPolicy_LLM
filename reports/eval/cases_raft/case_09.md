## REFERENCE
Article 360 of the Egyptian Civil Code addresses the legal effects of a delegation (where a third party assumes the original debtor's obligation), distinguishing the case where the parties expressly agree to substitute the new obligation for the old (true novation) from the default case where both obligations remain in force.

Article 360 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides:

* **Novation requires express agreement:** A delegation amounts to a novation by change of debtor only when the contracting parties agree to substitute a new obligation for the original obligation; without such agreement, no novation occurs.
* **Effect of valid novation:** When novation is agreed, the original debtor is liberated from the obligation to the creditor — but only if (a) the new obligation assumed by the new debtor is valid, and (b) the new debtor is not insolvent at the time of the delegation.
* **No presumption of novation:** Novation is never presumed in a case of delegation; in the absence of a clear agreement to novate, the default applies.
* **Default: both obligations coexist:** Without novation, the original obligation continues concurrently with the new obligation, giving the creditor two debtors instead of one — typically a stronger position.

Example: A buyer arranges for a friend to take over his car-loan obligation to a bank. If the bank, the friend, and the buyer all agree expressly to release the buyer and accept only the friend, that is a novation under Article 360 — the buyer is liberated. If they don't expressly agree to release the buyer, the buyer remains liable alongside the friend, and the bank can pursue either.

* **Why the distinction** — The Code distinguishes between situations where the new obligation replaces the old one (novation) and situations where the new obligation coexists with the old one (continuing obligations).

Under Article 360, this constitutes a novation by substitution, liberating the original contractor from the obligation to the client, but leaving the client with the new contractor as their obligor. Without an agreement, the original contractor remains bound to the client, and the new contractor assumes the original contractor's position.
