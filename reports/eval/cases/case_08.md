## REFERENCE (gold-polished by Gemini/Claude)
Article 524 of the Egyptian Civil Code sets the default liability rule for partnership debts: partners are liable only in proportion to their respective shares, not jointly and severally. It also reallocates an insolvent partner's share among the remaining partners according to their loss-shares.

Article 524 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides:

* **No automatic joint and several liability** — Without a contrary agreement, each partner is responsible only for their respective share in the partnership's debts.
* **The default is favourable to partners** — Compared with full joint and several liability, the rule limits each partner's exposure to creditors.
* **Parties may opt for stricter liability** — The default may be displaced by an express contractual clause stipulating joint and several liability, often demanded by creditors as a condition of credit.
* **Reallocation upon insolvency** — If one partner cannot pay their share because they are insolvent, that share does not vanish — it is shifted to the other partners.
* **Reallocation key** — The shift is made in proportion to each partner's share in the losses, which may match or differ from their share in profits or contributions.
* **Practical balance** — Ordinary partners are shielded from unlimited exposure to a single partner's collapse, but creditors are still made whole through internal reallocation.

Example: Two partners A (60% loss share) and B (40% loss share) plus C (insolvent) jointly owe 100,000 EGP. C's 33,333 EGP share is reallocated between A and B by their loss-shares 60:40, so A pays an extra 20,000 and B an extra 13,333, on top of their own original shares.

## MODEL PREDICTION
Article 524 of the Egyptian Civil Code establishes that a person who has lost a personal injury compensation claim against another party may recover from the insurer of that party's insurance policy.

Article 524 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides:

* **Insurance coverage**: A person who has lost a personal injury claim against another party can recover from the insurer of that party's insurance policy.
* **No direct recovery**: The insurer is not directly liable to the injured party; the insurer must first pay the claimant's compensation.
* **Proportionate payment**: The insurer pays the claimant's compensation in proportion to its own liability under the policy.
* **No double recovery**: The claimant cannot simultaneously recover from both the insurer and the other party (the defendant).
* **Recovery period**: The claimant must wait until the insurer has paid the full amount of the claim before recovering from the insurer.
* **Exception: third-party liability**: If the insurer is also liable to the injured party through third-party liability, the insurer may pay the claimant's compensation directly.

Example: A pedestrian is hit by a car. The pedestrian sues the driver, but the driver has no money. The pedestrian then sues the insurer of the driver's policy. Under Article 524, the pedestrian can recover from the insurer in proportion to the insurer's liability under the policy, but the insurer must pay the full amount before the pedestrian can recover.
