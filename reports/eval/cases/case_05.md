## REFERENCE (gold-polished by Gemini/Claude)
Article 1035 of the Egyptian Civil Code lays down two foundational rules for any valid mortgage under Egyptian law: the property mortgaged must be immovable (with limited statutory exceptions), and it must be described with enough precision in an authentic document that it can be identified and sold at auction.

Article 1035 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides:

* **Limitation to immovable property** — As a general rule, only immovable property (land and what is permanently affixed to it) may be the subject of a mortgage. Exceptions exist only where the law expressly says so.
* **Marketability requirement** — The property must be capable of being lawfully sold; assets that are inalienable or outside commerce cannot serve as security.
* **Auction-readiness** — The property must be capable of being sold by public auction, since auction is the standard method of realising the security if the debtor defaults.
* **Specificity of description** — The mortgage must describe the property precisely as to its nature (type, components) and its situation (location, boundaries), preventing later disputes about scope.
* **Authentic-document formality** — The required description must appear in the deed creating the mortgage, or in a later authentic (notarised) document. A description in a private writing is insufficient.
* **Sanction of nullity** — Failure to meet the description requirement renders the mortgage void, not merely voidable; the security disappears entirely.

Example: A bank lends money against a mortgage over "the borrower's land near Alexandria." If the deed does not pinpoint the parcel by area, boundaries, and registry reference in an authentic instrument, Article 1035 nullifies the mortgage and the bank loses its security, becoming an ordinary unsecured creditor.
