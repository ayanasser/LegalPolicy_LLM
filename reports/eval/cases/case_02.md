# Case 02 — lang=en, kind=explanation, article=Article 369

## PROMPT
What does Article 369 of the Egyptian Civil Code establish? Summarize it for a non-lawyer.

## ORIGINAL ARTICLE (EN)
A debtor who had the right to set up compensation but 
who nevertheless paid his debt, cannot avail himself, to 
the prejudice of third parties, of the securities 
guaranteeing his right unless he did not know of the 
existence of his right.

## REFERENCE (gold-polished by Gemini/Claude)
Article 369 of the Egyptian Civil Code addresses what happens when a debtor who could have set up compensation against his creditor nevertheless paid the debt, and limits his ability to later use the original securities to the prejudice of third parties — unless he was unaware of the right.

Article 369 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides:

* **Trigger: debtor had compensation right but paid:** The article applies to a debtor who had the right to set up compensation (set-off) against his creditor's claim but who chose to pay the debt instead.
* **General rule: cannot prejudice third parties:** Such a debtor cannot avail himself of the securities that guaranteed his (cross-) right against the original creditor to the prejudice of third parties (e.g., other creditors with junior rankings on the same security).
* **Exception: ignorance of the right:** This restriction does not apply if the debtor did not know of the existence of his cross-claim (and thus of his compensation right) at the time of payment; ignorance preserves his security position.
* **Underlying policy: protect third parties from voluntary loss of priority:** The rule prevents a debtor from voluntarily extinguishing a cross-claim and then trying to revive its securities at the expense of innocent third parties.
* **Burden of proof:** The debtor seeking to invoke the exception must prove his ignorance of the right at the relevant time.

Example: Alice owes Bob EGP 50,000; Bob owes Alice EGP 30,000 secured by a mortgage on his land. Alice pays Bob the full EGP 50,000 instead of setting off. Other junior creditors of Bob have liens on the same land. Under Article 369, Alice cannot now claim her EGP 30,000 secured priority to the prejudice of those junior creditors — unless she proves she did not know of her cross-claim when she paid.

## MODEL PREDICTION
Article 369 of the Egyptian Civil Code establishes that a person who has lost their right to a thing (e.g., because they have died) can transfer that right to another person, even if the original owner is still alive.

Article 369 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides:

* **Transferability of a lost right**: A person who has lost his right to a thing (e.g., because he has died) may transfer that right to another person.
* **Ownership continuity**: The transfer does not affect the ownership of the thing itself; the new owner acquires ownership in the same way as the original owner.
* **Right of succession**: The transferee becomes the new owner and inherits all rights associated with the thing, including any claims against third parties.
* **No need for consent**: The transfer is effective without the consent of the original owner, who remains liable for the transferred right.
* **Example**: A person dies intestate, leaving a house to their son. The son then transfers the right to the house to his friend, who becomes the new owner. The son's death does not affect the new owner's title to the house.
