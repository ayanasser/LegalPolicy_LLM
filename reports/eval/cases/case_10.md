# Case 10 — lang=en, kind=explanation, article=Article 670

## PROMPT
What does Article 670 of the Egyptian Civil Code establish? Summarize it for a non-lawyer.

## ORIGINAL ARTICLE (EN)
When the concessionaire of the public utility service 
enjoys a de jure or de facto monopoly service, he is 
bound to observe strict equality between consumers 
both as regards the services rendered and the rates 
charged. 
The principle of equality does not exclude special 
treatment involving the reduction of remittance of rates, 
provided such treatment is granted to all persons who 
apply therefor and who fulfill the general conditions laid 
down by the concessionaire. The principle of equality 
entail, however, the prohibition of the concessionaire 
from granting to some consumers advantages which he 
refuses to grant to others. 
Any discrimination granted contrary to the provisions of 
the preceding paragraph renders the concessionaire 
liable to compensation for the loss which may be caused, 
as a result of such discrimination to third parties, by the 
disturbance of the natural balance of fair competition.

## REFERENCE (gold-polished by Gemini/Claude)
Article 670 of the Egyptian Civil Code establishes a duty of equality on a public-utility concessionaire who enjoys a monopoly. They must treat all consumers equally — both in services rendered and in rates charged — but can grant generally available special programmes without violating the rule. Discriminatory advantages create liability.

Article 670 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides that, when a public-utility concessionaire enjoys a de jure or de facto monopoly, they must observe strict equality between consumers regarding services and rates; that special treatment such as rate reductions is allowed if granted to all who apply and meet the general conditions; that the concessionaire is prohibited from granting some consumers advantages refused to others; and that any prohibited discrimination renders the concessionaire liable to compensate third parties for losses caused by the disturbance of fair competition.

For a non-lawyer, the rule has three takeaways:

1. Strict equality is the default. A monopoly concessionaire (for example, a utility that has no real competition) must give all consumers the same services on the same terms — including price.

2. Generally available programmes are fine. Special tariff plans or discounts are not banned, as long as they are open to all who apply and meet the published conditions. Equality means equal access, not identical pricing for everyone.

3. Discriminatory advantages create liability. If the concessionaire grants some consumers advantages it refuses to others, it can be held liable in damages to third parties — typically competitors or other consumers — harmed by the resulting distortion of fair competition.

Example: A regional electricity concessionaire offers a discount to a single large factory while refusing the same terms to a similar competing factory. Under Article 670, the concessionaire breaches its equality duty and may be liable to the disadvantaged factory.
