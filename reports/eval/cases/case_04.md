## REFERENCE (gold-polished by Gemini/Claude)
Article 1076 of the Egyptian Civil Code addresses the chain of title that arises when an immovable burdened by a mortgage is sold at auction and a new buyer — not the third party who held the property — wins the auction. It clarifies that the new owner takes title from the third party holder, not from the original mortgagor.

Article 1076 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides that, in the preceding cases, if a person other than the third party holder acquires the property at the auction, that person will hold their right by virtue of the judgment of adjudication from the third party holder.

To walk through the article:

1. Context: enforcement against a third party holder. The article belongs to the rules governing the situation where a mortgaged immovable has been transferred to a third party (the "third party holder") and the mortgagee creditor enforces the mortgage. The "preceding cases" refer to the procedural scenarios already described in the surrounding articles.

2. A new buyer acquires at auction. When the property is sold at auction in the course of enforcement, the buyer (the auction winner, sometimes called the adjudicataire) is often someone different from the third party holder.

3. Title flows from the third party holder, not the original mortgagor. The article specifies that the new buyer's title is derived — by virtue of the judgment of adjudication — from the third party holder. This determines the legal lineage of the property and affects subsequent claims, warranties, and the chain of registration.

Example: A buys mortgaged land from B, becoming a third party holder. The mortgagee creditor enforces; the land is auctioned; C wins the auction. Under Article 1076, C's title is derived from A (the third party holder) through the judgment of adjudication, not directly from B (the original mortgagor).

## MODEL PREDICTION
Article 1076 of the Egyptian Civil Code establishes the rule that when two or more persons jointly own property, each has an equal right to the property and the right to dispose of it in any way they choose.

Article 1076 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides:

* **Joint ownership**: Two or more persons jointly own property.
* **Equal right**: Each owner has an equal right to the property.
* **Dispositional power**: Each owner has the right to dispose of the property in any way they choose.

Example: A family owns a house together. Each family member has an equal share of the house's value and can sell, mortgage, or otherwise transfer their share as they see fit.
