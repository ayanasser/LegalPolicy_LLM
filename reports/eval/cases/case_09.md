## REFERENCE (gold-polished by Gemini/Claude)
Article 318 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides that an assigned debt is transmitted to the assignee with all its warranties — but personal sureties or real guarantors of the debt do not remain bound to the new creditor unless they have agreed to the assignment.

Article 318 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides:

* **Transmission with warranties** — When a debt is assigned, the warranties (privileges, mortgages, and similar accessory rights) accompany the debt to the new creditor automatically.
* **Why warranties follow the debt** — Warranties are accessory to the debt; without them, the debt would change in economic substance, contrary to the parties' expectations.
* **Exception: sureties' need to consent** — A surety (whether personal — like a guarantor — or real — like a third-party mortgagor) does not remain bound to the assignee unless they have agreed to the assignment.
* **Why surety consent matters** — Sureties bind themselves based on trust in a particular creditor; allowing the creditor to be replaced without their consent would change the relationship's character and risk.
* **Practical implication for assignees** — Assignees should obtain the surety's consent during the assignment process to preserve the full security package.
* **Practical implication for sureties** — They retain the right to refuse to be passed to a new creditor; this preserves their bargaining position.
* **Effect of refusal** — Without consent, the debt is transmitted without the surety; the assignee can still collect from the debtor but loses the security.

Example: A creditor assigns a 500,000 EGP debt secured by a guarantee from the debtor's father to a new financier. Under Article 318, the debt and any registered mortgage transfer automatically; the father's guarantee, however, only transfers if he consents to the assignment.

## MODEL PREDICTION
Article 318 of the Egyptian Civil Code establishes that when a person has no legal representative or guardian, their heirs have the right to act on their behalf in certain circumstances.

Article 318 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides:

* **Right of succession**: The heirs of a deceased person have the right to act on his behalf in certain situations.
* **Specificity**: This right is limited to certain acts, not all actions.
* **Purpose**: It allows the heirs to represent the deceased in cases where he cannot do so himself, such as when he is incapacitated or lacks legal capacity.
* **Limitation**: The heirs' authority is subject to the same conditions as those of the deceased's legal representative or guardian — they must act in good faith and with due diligence.
* **Example**: A child inherits an estate but cannot manage it because of a mental illness. The child's siblings can act on his behalf to sell the property, provided they follow the proper procedures and act in good faith.
