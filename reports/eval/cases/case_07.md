## PROMPT
What does Article 848 of the Egyptian Civil Code establish? Summarize it for a non-lawyer.

## REFERENCE (gold-polished by Gemini/Claude)
Article 848 of the Egyptian Civil Code establishes the legal framework for a "provisional partition" — a non-final division of jointly owned property — by borrowing from the rules on lease contracts. The result is a familiar set of rules adapted to the partition's temporary nature.

Article 848 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides that a provisional partition is governed — as to its validity against third parties, the capacity of co-partitioners, their rights and obligations, and means of proof — by the provisions of the law relating to contracts of lease, insofar as they are not incompatible with the nature of such a partition.

For a non-lawyer, the rule has three takeaways:

1. Provisional partitions are recognised. Co-owners do not have to choose between full ongoing co-ownership and a final, irrevocable partition. They can opt for a temporary division of use and enjoyment.

2. Lease rules apply by analogy. Because the provisional partition gives each co-owner the use of a specific part for a defined period, it strongly resembles a lease. The article borrows lease rules for four key areas: validity against third parties, capacity, mutual rights and duties, and proof.

3. Compatibility check. The lease rules apply only "insofar as they are not incompatible" with the provisional partition's nature. Where a particular lease rule does not fit, it does not apply.

Example: Two siblings own a duplex jointly. They agree to provisionally divide it — each takes one floor for three years. Under Article 848, lease-style rules govern their relationship for those three years, including how third parties are affected and how their respective rights and duties are proved.

## MODEL PREDICTION
Article 848 of the Egyptian Civil Code establishes that the owner of a vehicle may be held liable for damage caused to a third party by the vehicle's occupants if the owner knew or should have known that the occupants were likely to cause harm.

Article 848 of the Egyptian Civil Code provides:

* **Liability for occupant-caused damage** — The owner of a vehicle is liable for damage caused by its occupants to a third party if the owner knew or should have known that the occupants were likely to cause harm.
* **Reasonable knowledge requirement** — The owner must have had reasonable knowledge of the occupants' likely actions, not just general knowledge of the occupants' presence.
* **Proportionality principle** — The owner's liability is limited to the extent that the damage was caused by the occupants' actions, not the total damage suffered.
* **Example** — A car owner who knows their passenger is prone to aggressive driving could be held liable for damage caused by that behavior, but only up to the amount of damage caused by the aggressive driving itself.
* **Limitations** — The owner's liability does not extend to damage caused by the vehicle itself (e.g., collision with another vehicle) or damage caused by the vehicle's inherent defects.
